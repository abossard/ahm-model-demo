# Timeline history is kept by model and tile, recorded while watched and read ahead

The app keeps the closed history of a model in one blob per tile of the browser (ADR 0009), under `history/v2/<subscription>/<resource group>/<model>/`: `overview/<week>.json` holds the transitions and annotations of every entity for a week, and `series/<slot seconds>/<tile>.json` the points of every signal at one level: every evaluation (`0`) and slots of 15 minutes (`900`) by the day, slots of an hour (`3600`) by the week, thinned by the same `decimate` as an answer. Inside a blob, a piece is what one Azure read gives, a signal for a day or the transitions or annotations of an entity for a week, kept only whole, as the rows an answer carries, with where it came from. A closed tile at any level is one blob read and is answered as stored, without thinning. Memory holds 64 MB of blobs, least recently used out. A blob is written after the answer has gone, by a few writers in the background, over the version it was merged onto, so two writers keep both their pieces. Three things fill the store. A request reads a missing piece from Azure, as before, and keeps it at every level. The live hub (ADR 0007), which now polls at a fixed rate and writes in a task of its own, records what its polls see, the latest evaluation of every signal and the transitions it reads when a state changes, and hands a day (or a week) over once it has closed. It does so only when its polls covered all of it and the 15 minutes after it, with no poll ending more than one and a half poll intervals after the previous one began; only for signals evaluated at most once every two poll intervals, so a poll sees every evaluation, that were there at every poll, never evaluated sooner than their refresh interval says and not on a day the app applied changes to the model; and for transitions only when every change it saw in the week was found in that entity's history by a read that began before the change. Anything else is left to Azure, whole. A piece read from Azure replaces a recorded one and never the other way round, so a recording never stands in for finer points. Last, opening a model's timeline starts a fill in the background that reads every missing closed piece of the 30 days, newest first, with the opener's own sign-in and only after the access check every route makes: one fill a model, 2 calls at a time, 4 at a time and 300 a minute across the app, only while no interactive call waits, and it stops 2 minutes before the sign-in expires, at a 429 that three waits do not clear, after three failures in a row, or after 8000 calls. The first layout (`history/v1`, one blob per history and bucket) is still read where the new one has nothing, and what is found there is written into it. Every timeline request reports its Azure calls, its stored and read pieces and its blob reads on its span and in one log line.

Before this, a closed bucket was one blob per signal and day, so the month of a model of 100 signals was 3000 blob reads, the coarse levels were thinned from every point on each read, and the first viewer after a quiet spell paid the 3000 or more Azure calls in their own requests. The browser asks tile by tile, so a blob per tile is what it asks for. The costs: a blob holds every signal of the model, about 3 MB a day of every point for 100 signals evaluated every minute, and is rewritten whole when a piece is added, so a cold day of a large model costs a few large writes and a request for one signal reads the day of all of them; memory holds only a few days of every point of a large model; the recording relies on the snapshot's latest evaluation being the newest point of the history (checked on the live model: the same time, value and state) and on 15 minutes being enough for a late evaluation, as closing a tile already does; a state that changes and changes back between two polls, with no other change of that entity, is missing from a recorded week, which is why a new health report has its entity's history read too; a signal evaluated out of turn that the hub never saw early (two evaluations between two polls) still passes, which the day of an Apply is excluded for; a recording lives only as long as its hub, so a restart or a quiet hour gives that day to Azure; a hub holds at most 300,000 points, so a day of a model beyond that is not recorded; and a fill spends the opener's Azure quota on days nobody may look at.

Rejected: one blob per signal and day as before (3000 reads for a month, and nowhere to keep the levels); thinning only when serving (every overview read pays for every point); keeping the hourly level by the day and reading seven blobs for a week tile (a tile would no longer be one read, and making the browser's tile a day would throw away every tile browsers hold); filling the gaps of a partly recorded day from Azure (a gap costs the same call as the day); letting a recorded day win over Azure's (it can be coarser where a poll was late); recording annotations (the hub reads them every 5 minutes for the last 15, and a week of them costs one call an entity); a collector on the app's identity (ADR 0006); filling every model of the catalog (it reads what nobody opened); and answering with a blob's bytes as stored (an answer names some entities and signals of a tile, a blob holds them all).
